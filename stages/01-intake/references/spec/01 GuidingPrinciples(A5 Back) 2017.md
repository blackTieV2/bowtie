## Guiding Principles

Is a deviation from the desired state or activity
Happens before major damage has occurred
It is still possible to recover
Hazards can have multiple Top Events
E.g.: Losing control over the car, loss of (hydrocarbons) containment,
deviation from intended ﬂight path
Describes the desired state or activity
Is part of normal business
Has the potential to cause harm if control is lost
Use guidewords to describe hazards
(i.e. energy, name, state and context)
E.g.: Driving a car, hydrocarbons in containment,
landing an aircraft
Prevent, control or mitigate undesired events or accidents
Can be (a combination of) behaviour and hardware
A Barrier System contains a detect, decide & act component
E.g.: Wearing a seatbelt, Blow-Out Preventer,
Ground Proximity Warning System
Eliminates the Threat or prevents the Top Event
Reduces the impact of the outcome or mitigates the severity
of the  Consequence
Reduces the eﬀect of the Defeating Factor
Are factors that reduce the eﬀectiveness of Controls
Should be used sparingly to highlight real issues
Focus on critical Barriers
Avoid repetition and duplication
E.g.: Forgeting to wear the seatbelt, no maintenance done,
person not trained
Are the unwanted events arising from the Top Event
Assists identify the targets aﬀected or being protected
Describes the direct cause for  the injury, loss or damage
E.g.: Car rollover, ignition of vapor cloud, mid-air collision
Are credible causes for the Top Event
Are not Control failures
Should lead directly to the Top Event
Should be able to lead independently to the Top Event
E.g.: Driving on a slippery road, pipeline corrosion,
loss of positional awareness
