###### Process steps Process guidance HAZARD Allocate the hazard a code

###### Name the hazard in summary form Describe the hazard in detail

###### Record what the hazard affects (HSE) Allocate a responsible party

###### Check for understanding & questions Hazard identification (HAZID) is actually a separate

###### process to BowTie analysis and should be conducted in a different study.

###### Hazards are a part of normal business. Hazards are usually a source of energy.

###### Hazards are not events that happen, they are things that exist.

###### Try to be specific with the way you ‘name’ the

###### hazard so it is clear and agreed to by all.

###### A ‘generic’ hazard will be harder to analyse later on.

###### Try to be specific now. Use the hazard description to provide additional

###### information relating to the nature and potential of the hazard.

###### Process steps Process guidance TOP EVENT Name the Top Event in summary form

###### Describe the Top Event in detail Check for understanding & questions

###### The Top Event should not be a disaster or an event

###### that actually causes any harm. ‘Top ‘ usually means the first significant event in a

###### chain of events of increasing potential for harm.

###### Top Events usually involve a change of state of the

###### hazard. Check to be sure that there isn’t more than one Top

###### Event for this hazard? Try to be specific with the way you ‘name’ the Top

###### Event so it is clear and agreed by all what it is, and

###### what it is not. If you are not sure at the moment, just record ‘Loss

###### of Control’ and come back to it later. Card No. 22

###### Process steps Process guidance OUTCOMES Name the Outcome in summary form

###### Describe the Outcome in detail Repeat until no more Outcomes exist

###### Check for understanding & questions Outcomes are events that happen and they are not

###### the actual harm that they cause. Consider different Outcomes for different risk

###### targets.  E.G. People Outcomes, Asset Outcomes, Environmental Outcomes.

###### Try to think about outcomes as multiple independent

###### events that escalate and not just the final disastrous event at the end.

###### Be prepared to go back and rename any previous

###### Outcomes as you learn more about the event tree.

###### If you think of a control during this step, quickly add

###### it into the model so it is not forgotten later.

###### Process steps Process guidance CAUSES Name the Cause in summary form

###### Describe the Cause in detail Repeat until no more Causes exist

###### Check for understanding & questions Causes should be ‘direct’ and a ‘sufficient cause’ of

###### the Top Event. Controls failing are not Causes.

###### Causes should usually be named as something that is unwanted and thus can be targeted for

###### elimination. Be specific; Don’t just write Corrosion.  What causes

###### the corrosion and what is corroded. Be prepared to go back and rename any previous

###### Causes as you learn more about the fault tree.

###### If you think of a control during this step, quickly add

###### it into the model so it is not forgotten later.

###### Process steps Process guidance CONTROLS Name the Control in summary form

###### Describe the Control in detail Assess the Controls type

###### Allocate the Controls responsibility Repeat until no more controls exist

###### Repeat for each branch in the BowTie Check for understanding & questions

###### Identify controls by applying the pre-determined hierarchy of control.

###### Controls should be ordered in accordance with a pre-

###### determined scheme.  The type of control is often used for this.

###### Try to be consistent with the way that controls are

###### named. Do not be verbose when naming controls.  Try to

###### keep names to three lines.  Descriptions can be as long as you like.

###### Be prepared to go back an alter your Causes and

###### Process steps Process guidance DEFEATING FACTORS Name Defeating Factor in summary form

###### Describe the Defeating Factor in detail Repeat until no more Defeating Factors

###### exist Complete the Control step for each new Defeating Factor

###### Check for understanding & questions Defeating factors need to be significant or credible.

###### Prior incidents are a indication of the credibility of defeating factors.

###### Analytical effort for defeating factors should be focused on the critical controls.

###### Defeating factors should be identified by considering

###### the human factors, mechanical failures, abnormal

###### environmental or operational conditions and the failure of critical services.

###### Defeating Factors should not cause the Top Event to

###### occur.  If they do, then they should be a Cause.
